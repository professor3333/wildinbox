# Review study 1: what the results show

The pre-registered analysis is in [README.md](README.md) and
[metrics.json](metrics.json), generated by `wildinbox study analyze` and not
edited. This file adds the summary without the author, which was declared in
[PLAN.md](PLAN.md) before any results were seen, and checks that were chosen
**after** seeing the results. The post-hoc checks are labelled as such.

Five participants took part: the author and four volunteers (not trained field
reviewers). The protocol needs 8, so none of this is a verdict. All numbers
are descriptive.

## Pre-registered: all participants (author flagged)

| | Grouped | Suggested | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median seconds per event | 2.5 s | 1.1 s | -1.4 s [-3.8, +0.5] |
| Mean accuracy | 48% | 43% | -4.2 points [-17.5, +9.1] |

Three of five participants were faster with suggestions. The workload
projection in README.md (57% time saved) is computed from these medians.

## Pre-declared: without the author

| | Grouped | Suggested | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median seconds per event | 2.4 s | 1.9 s | -0.6 s [-1.6, +0.5] |
| Mean accuracy | 48% | 45% | -3.4 points [-20.0, +13.2] |

Two of four volunteers were faster with suggestions. The same projection
gives 21% time saved.

In both summaries the intervals for time and accuracy include zero.

## Post-hoc: participants mostly accepted the suggestion

The suggestions shown were right on **28 of the 80** timed events (35%).
24 of the 80 are unsupported species (squirrel, bird, rodent), where the
correct answer is "other species", which the model never suggests. On the 56
supported events, it was right on 28 (50%).

How often each participant's answer matched the suggestion. In the grouped
condition, participants did not see the suggestion, so that column shows how
often they agree with the model anyway:

| Participant | Suggested block: matches suggestion | Grouped block: matches model | Accuracy, grouped / suggested | Median s, grouped / suggested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| author | 40 / 40 | 13 / 40 | 45% / 38% | 4.6 / 0.7 |
| P1 | 39 / 40 | 22 / 40 | 50% / 32% | 2.7 / 1.1 |
| P2 | 40 / 40 | 21 / 40 | 55% / 32% | 2.2 / 0.8 |
| P3 | 22 / 40 | 13 / 40 | 38% / 55% | 2.2 / 2.7 |
| P4 | 23 / 40 | 16 / 40 | 50% / 59% | 2.5 / 2.7 |

Two patterns:
- **The author, P1 and P2 accepted nearly every suggestion.** They took about
  a second per event, and their accuracy fell to roughly the model's own
  level. Nearly all of the pre-registered speed gain comes from these three.
- **P3 and P4 used the suggestions selectively.** They were not faster, but
  they were more accurate with suggestions than without (+17 and +9 points).

The observed speed gain is not evidence that suggestions help reviewers. In
this study, it measures how fast people can accept a model that was wrong
most of the time.

## Post-hoc: unsupported species were missed in both conditions

Unsupported species were 60 decisions per condition (12 events × 5
participants). Participants answered "other species" 3 times without
suggestions and never with them. Without suggestions, they labelled 42 of the
60 as empty. With suggestions, they mostly took the suggested species. Empty
events were recognised well: 33/35 without suggestions, 29/35 with them.
Median decisions of 1-3 seconds suggest small animals in these frames were
often not seen at all.

## What this means

- The study does not support claiming reviewer time savings from
  suggestions. The 57% projection should not be quoted as a benefit.
- The model's suggestions on these calibration-camera events (35% correct,
  50% on supported species) are too weak to be accepted without checking.
  Accepting them without checking is exactly what three of five
  participants did.
- More participants would not change the main problem. A next study is worth
  running only after the suggestions become more reliable, or once the
  interface stops a single click from accepting an unchecked label.
