# Feasibility: automatic labels at 95% precision (development only)

Source: the released model's development evaluation (`models/finetune-e3-deep-balanced/eval/events.jsonl.gz`), event suggestions and their uncalibrated confidence (a cutoff depends only on the ranking). The final test is not read. Generated by [`scripts/coverage_frontier.py`](../../../scripts/coverage_frontier.py); numbers in [`frontier.json`](frontier.json).

Share of **all** events that could be labelled automatically while precision stays >= 95%:

| Partition | Events | Empty | One cutoff | Per label, in-sample | Per label, lower bound | Perfect selector |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seen cameras (held-out sequences of training cameras) | 1,746 | 5.8% | 25.2% | 42.6% | 16.0% | 64.4% |
| Unseen: calibration cameras 51, 108 | 908 | 20.3% | 1.2% | 16.4% | 0.0% | 41.7% |
| Unseen: policy-validation cameras 90, 125 | 939 | 9.2% | 1.6% | 9.6% | 0.0% | 48.0% |

- **One cutoff:** a single confidence threshold for every suggestion.
- **Per label, in-sample:** the best threshold per suggested label, chosen on these same events, so optimistic.
- **Per label, lower bound:** the 95% Wilson lower bound of precision must itself reach 95%, as the release rule requires.
- **Perfect selector:** every correct suggestion accepted and nothing else; no threshold on this model can do better.

## What this shows

- On unseen cameras even a perfect selector over this model's suggestions labels under half the events, and no label passes the release rule's lower bound. The 50% review-reduction target cannot be met by tuning thresholds.
- On cameras the model was trained on, the in-sample bound approaches but does not reach 50%. This is the most that adapting the model to a camera could plausibly deliver with this architecture, and it motivates the v2 experiment: adapt to each new camera from its first reviewed events, and accept labels only where that camera's precision holds.
